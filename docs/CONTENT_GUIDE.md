# Content Creation Guide

This document describes how to create content that fits the systemic design.

## NPC Design Template

Every important NPC should answer:

### Identity

- Name
- Occupation
- Age bracket
- Wealth class
- Home/location

### Personality

Choose 4–8 strong traits rather than 20 generic traits.

Examples:

- trusting
- suspicious
- vain
- lonely
- greedy
- empathetic
- stubborn
- anxious
- curious
- status-conscious
- superstitious
- proud

### Values

Examples:

- family
- money
- status
- honesty
- religion
- knowledge
- security
- adventure
- community

### Goals

Goals create leverage.

Bad:

> "Wants to be happy."

Good:

> "Wants to save 5,000 Kurtos to reopen her late husband's workshop."

### Fears

Fears create vulnerabilities and conversation hooks.

### Secrets

Only meaningful secrets should exist.

### Scam Resistance

Describe why the NPC is easy or difficult to manipulate.

Do not simply assign a difficulty number.

---

## Item Design

Items should create new approaches, not only bigger numbers.

Good item:

> Opens a new social identity or conversation angle.

Less interesting item:

> +5% trust to everyone.

Items should have situational strengths and weaknesses.

---

## Scam Design

Every scam needs:

```text
Setup
Target
Pitch opportunity
Prerequisites
Reward
Risk
Failure consequence
Possible follow-up
```

A good scam often has 2–4 steps.

Example:

```text
Find merchant
   ↓
Gain trust
   ↓
Learn merchant fears bankruptcy
   ↓
Acquire fake investment papers
   ↓
Convince merchant of limited-time opportunity
   ↓
Collect investment
   ↓
Choose to disappear / repay / double down
```

---

## Dialogue Writing

NPC replies should:

- sound like the individual character;
- react to what the player actually said;
- avoid exposition dumps;
- remain short enough for gameplay;
- preserve known facts;
- create room for the player to continue the conversation.

Avoid every NPC replying with:

> "I understand your concern."

That is generic and repetitive.

---

## Humor

Humor should come from character and situation rather than random memes.

Examples:

- an extremely suspicious guard who is somehow convinced by a ridiculous story because it appeals to his pride;
- a poor beggar with better knowledge of the town than the mayor;
- a merchant falling for an investment scam because he desperately wants to look smarter than his competitor.

---

## Avoid Content That Breaks the Simulation

Do not create NPCs who:

- magically know everything;
- have infinite money;
- instantly trust the player for no reason;
- forget major events without a mechanic explaining it;
- contradict established relationships;
- produce arbitrary world-changing objects during conversation.

---

## Content Scaling

Start with:

- 5–8 core NPCs;
- 1–2 houses each;
- 3–5 basic scams;
- 5–10 useful items;
- 2–3 connected secrets.

Only expand after the interaction loop is fun.
